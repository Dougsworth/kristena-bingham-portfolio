01

The problem

Getting around Kingston means juggling bus routes, route taxis that leave when they’re full, and cash fares.

No single app shows all three together, so people rely on habit and asking around.

  • 01
    Two ways to travel, planned separately

    JUTC buses run on routes and times. Route taxis run from stands. They’re how many Kingstonians actually travel, yet they’re left out of trip planning.

  • 02
    No shared sense of time

    A bus in 6 minutes and a taxi that leaves when it fills are hard to compare in your head.

  • 03
    Fares add up quietly

    Cash fares across a week are easy to lose track of.

02

Who it’s for

Daily commuters in Kingston who mix JUTC buses and route taxis: people heading to work in Half Way Tree and New Kingston, and students going to UWI Mona.

Design goals

  • 01
    Answer “what’s leaving now?” first

    Nearby departures before you type anything.

  • 02
    Compare every way on one scale

    Buses and route taxis side by side, in minutes you can see.

  • 03
    Stay calm on board

    Count down stops and buzz before yours.

  • 04
    Talk about fares in trips

    Show what a balance is worth in rides.

03

How it works

Plan, compare, ride.

  1. Step 01

    Open the app

    Nearby departures and your saved places, with door-to-door times right now.

  2. Step 02

    Pick where you’re going

    Home, work and UWI Mona are one tap away.

  3. Step 03

    Compare three ways

    Recommended, fastest and cheapest, drawn to the same time scale.

  4. Step 04

    Ride

    The live trip counts down stops, not minutes.

  5. Step 05

    Get off

    It buzzes one stop before yours. Share the trip with whoever’s waiting.

  6. Step 06

    Top up

    The fare card shows the balance as trips, and what you spent this week.

    06

    Key design decisions

    Route taxis belong in the plan

    They’re how many Kingstonians actually travel, so they sit beside JUTC buses instead of being left out.

    Draw time, don’t just list it

    Walking, waiting and riding share one scale, so the quickest option stands out before you read a single number.

    Count stops, not minutes

    On board, the app counts down stops and buzzes one before yours, which is what riders actually watch for.

    Fares in trips, not just dollars

    The fare card shows how many rides the balance covers, so topping up is an easy call.

    Always offer a backup

    A slower route with a change is still shown, marked as the backup, for when the first choice doesn’t work out.

    Be honest about route taxis

    They don’t run to a timetable, so the app says “leaves when full” and gives an estimate instead of a made-up departure time.

    07

    Visual language

    Deep greens and a warm cream keep it calm.

    Yellow is saved for the moments that matter on board: the arrival time, the trip’s progress and the buzz. A heavy, rounded display face makes times and stop counts readable at a glance.

    Status and next steps

    Done so far
    • Five core screens designed in Figma: welcome, nearby departures, route options, live trip and fare card
    • Sample routes, fares and times throughout
    Not yet
    • Interviews with daily commuters
    • Testing the to-scale route bars against a plain list
    • A working build with real transit data, so there’s no live link yet

    What I’d test next

    • The test

      Interview daily commuters about how they plan trips today, then test whether the to-scale route bars help people choose faster than a plain list.

    • What I’d watch

      Time to choose, and whether people pick the option that fits the trip they were given.

    • Open questions

      Where would reliable bus and route taxi data come from? How should the app estimate a stand that’s slow to fill?
