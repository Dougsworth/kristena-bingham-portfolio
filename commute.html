01

Background

JUTC commuters rely on route and schedule information to plan journeys around the Kingston Metropolitan Transport Region.

That information exists. Kingston Commute explores how JUTC journey planning could come together in one mobile-first experience that combines route discovery, schedules and, potentially, real-time vehicle information.

Why only JUTC

I deliberately narrowed the project to JUTC buses rather than Jamaica’s wider public transport system. Route taxis don’t run to the same fixed schedules, which makes reliable journey and arrival tracking a very different problem.

02

The problem

Commuters need clear, accessible information to decide which bus to take, where to board and when it should arrive.

Published schedules help people plan, but a schedule alone can’t say where a bus is right now, or when its arrival has changed.

How might we

How might we help JUTC commuters plan their journeys and reduce uncertainty around bus arrivals through accessible scheduling information and live tracking?

03

Discovery

The first research phase looks at the current JUTC route and schedule experience alongside established transit apps.

What the research explores

  • 01

    How commuters currently find routes and schedules.

  • 02

    What information they need when planning a journey.

  • 03

    How route, stop and departure information should be organised.

  • 04

    How transit apps tell scheduled and real-time information apart.

  • 05

    How the interface should respond when live information is delayed or unavailable.

Comparative analysis

Google Maps, Moovit and Transit, looking closely at journey planning, nearby departures, route information and live tracking.

04

Key opportunities

Discovery pointed to four areas worth exploring further.

  • 01
    Journey planning

    Help commuters find suitable JUTC routes from where they are to where they need to go.

  • 02
    Accessible schedules

    Make published route and departure information easier to find and read on a phone.

  • 03
    Live tracking

    Explore how reported bus locations and estimated arrival times could help commuters make better decisions.

  • 04
    Transparent information

    Clearly separate scheduled departures, estimated arrivals and live vehicle information.

05

The solution

A mobile app concept built around three connected experiences.

  • 01
    Journey planning

    Enter a starting point and destination, and explore the JUTC routes that fit.

  • 02
    Bus schedules

    Search routes, see directions and stops, check published departure times and save the routes you use most.

  • 03
    Live bus tracking

    See a selected bus’s reported position and estimated arrival, when live information is available.

The first UX prototype will use simulated tracking. Real-time tracking in a working product would depend on access to JUTC vehicle-location data.

06

Key user flows

Planning a journey, and tracking a bus.

Planning a journey

  1. Step 01

    Enter destination

    Where you’re starting and where you’re going.

  2. Step 02

    Review routes

    The JUTC routes that make the trip.

  3. Step 03

    Compare options

    Time, departures and transfers side by side.

  4. Step 04

    Select a route

    Pick the one that suits.

  5. Step 05

    Check the details

    Where to board, the stops and any transfer.

Tracking a bus

  1. Step 01

    Open a route

    From search or saved routes.

  2. Step 02

    Choose direction and stop

    Which way, and where you’ll board.

  3. Step 03

    See upcoming departures

    What’s leaving next from that stop.

  4. Step 04

    Select a bus

    The one you plan to catch.

  5. Step 05

    Location and arrival

    Where it is, and when it should reach you.

The tracking experience also explores less-than-ideal conditions, like delayed location updates or tracking that isn’t available.

07

Designing for uncertainty

Live transport information raises a real UX challenge: the interface has to say how reliable the information is.

Rather than presenting every arrival time as equally certain, the design separates three kinds of information.

  • Scheduled

    Based on the published timetable.

  • Estimated

    A predicted arrival, based on the real-time information available.

  • Live

    Vehicle-location information being received right now.

If live tracking drops out, the app should say so plainly and fall back to the scheduled information, rather than showing stale information as if it were current.

Screens

The high-fidelity screens, JUTC only: planning a journey, tracking a bus, and what the app does when live information fails.

09

Testing and iteration

To be completed.

Primary research with JUTC commuters will look at how they currently plan journeys, find schedules, respond to uncertain arrival times and decide what information they need while travelling.

The prototype will then be tested with realistic tasks, like finding a bus for an unfamiliar journey and working out when a selected bus is expected to arrive.

10

Reflection

Kingston Commute began as a broader public transport concept, and was narrowed to JUTC services to make a more focused, feasible design problem.

Exploring live tracking also raised questions beyond interface design, like data availability and technical feasibility. This case study focuses on how commuters would interact with that information, while recognising that a real product would need access to the right operational data.

Routes, fares and times are sample data.
