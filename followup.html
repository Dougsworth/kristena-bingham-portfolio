Overview

Inspired By The Day To Day Of An Early Stage CRM

FollowUp was inspired by my experience using an early-stage internal CRM while supporting client management and operational workflows.

Through daily use, I ran into issues with task ownership, status visibility, navigation and communication between team members. Tasks that needed review or more context also showed how important it is to keep discussions and decisions connected to the work itself.

I used these experiences as the starting point for exploring a simpler way for small teams to create, manage and collaborate on client-related tasks.

Problem

Context Gets Lost When Updates Live In Too Many Places

Small teams often coordinate many client-related tasks across different people. When task updates, discussions and decisions are scattered, keeping context and working out what needs to happen next takes more effort than it should.

How might we

How might we help small teams quickly capture tasks, collaborate on next steps, and keep a clear record of discussions and updates in one place?

Discovery

Starting From What I Saw Firsthand

I began with observations from my own professional experience and secondary research into existing task-management and CRM products.

Where I started

  1. 01Less effort to capture

    Reducing the effort it takes to create and update tasks.

  2. 02Discussion stays with the task

    Keeping feedback and discussions connected to individual tasks.

  3. 03Easier to read at a glance

    Making client activity, task ownership and progress easier to understand.

Comparative analysis

I looked at Todoist, Asana, ClickUp and HubSpot to see how established products handle quick task creation, task discussions, approvals and client activity histories.

Opportunities

Four Areas Worth Exploring Further

  1. 01Quick task capture

    Create a task with a simple one-line entry instead of filling in a long form straight away.

  2. 02Contextual collaboration

    Keep comments, feedback and review requests inside the task they’re about.

  3. 03Clear progress

    Make ownership and task states, To Do, In Progress, Awaiting Review and Completed, easy to identify.

  4. 04Centralised client history

    Connect individual tasks and important updates to a chronological client record.

Solution

Capture It Fast, Keep The Conversation Attached

A lightweight client and task manager built on two ideas: capture what needs to happen quickly, and keep the collaboration that follows attached to that work.

  1. 01Personal dashboard

    A focused view of your assigned tasks and their current status.

  2. 02One-line log

    Capture a follow-up quickly, then review or change the task details it picked out.

  3. 03Task discussion

    A dedicated space for comments, feedback, review requests and relevant updates.

  4. 04Client timeline

    A chronological history of the important tasks and activity for each client.

  5. 05Contextual notifications

    A notification takes you straight to the task that needs your attention.

Key Flow

When A Follow Up Needs Someone Else’s Input

  1. Step 01

    Create the task

    Log the follow-up in a line.

  2. Step 02

    Prepare the follow-up

    Draft what’s going to the client.

  3. Step 03

    Request review

    Ask a teammate for input, from inside the task.

  4. Step 04

    Reviewer is notified

    The notification opens the task itself.

  5. Step 05

    Feedback or approval

    Given in the task’s discussion, next to the work.

  6. Step 06

    Complete the task

    The follow-up goes out and the task closes.

  7. Step 07

    Kept in the client’s history

    The task and its discussion stay on the client timeline.

This flow became central to the design because finishing a task isn’t always a solo action. Sometimes progress depends on another person’s decision or context.

Screens

The First Round Of Concept Screens

The first round: the personal dashboard, the one-line log, the task list, the client record and the phone follow-up. Task discussion, review requests and notifications are designed next.

Screen 01

Personal Dashboard

A focused view of your assigned tasks and where each one stands, with what’s late at the front.

  1. Straight to the point. A greeting and one line, then the work.
  2. Overdue leads. Twelve overdue follow-ups come first, in red, before anything that’s only due.
  3. The rest of the week, counted. Due today, this week and new leads, in the order they need you.
  4. Overdue first, by default. The task list is sorted by what’s late, with how long each one has waited.
  5. Priority you can scan. High, Medium and Low in red, amber and green, the same on every screen.
  6. Ownership in the open. Team activity shows who assigned what to whom, next to notes and updates.
Screen 02

One Line Log

Capture a follow-up in a line, then review or change what it understood.

  1. Say it naturally. Write it the way you’d tell a colleague: who, what they want, what happens next.
  2. Call, meeting, email or note. The type is set from what you wrote, and one tap changes it.
  3. Shows what it understood. Before anything is saved, every field it pulled out is listed, starting with the contact it matched.
  4. A summary for the record. The note is cut down to one line for the client’s history.
  5. The next step, with a date. “Send him a proposal and follow up next week” becomes Send proposal, due next week. Every row can be opened and fixed.
  6. One save. The note, the next step and the due date go in together.
Screen 03

Task List

Every open follow-up, with what’s late at the top. The next round adds the four task states: To Do, In Progress, Awaiting Review and Completed.

  1. New task, one tap. The plus button starts a follow-up from the list.
  2. Filter by urgency. All, Overdue, Today and This week, with counts on the two that matter most.
  3. Tick to finish. The circle is the checkbox, so one tap marks a task done.
  4. Colour means priority. The circle and the tag share red, amber or green, so the list can be scanned without reading every line.
  5. How long it’s waited. Time sits on the right, and the arrow opens the task.
Screen 04

Client Record

Everything about one client. The activity tab is where the client timeline, a chronological record of tasks and updates, will live.

  1. Who, and where they stand. Company, contact and role, with Lead and High priority tags in the same colours as the task board.
  2. Act from the record. Call, email, meet or add a note without leaving the page.
  3. Five tabs, one client. Overview, activity, tasks, deals and files.
  4. The basics, plainly. Company, industry, location and website.
  5. The latest note, on top. What was logged on the last call, with who wrote it and when.
Screen 05

After The Call

On a phone, straight after hanging up.

  1. Out of the way fast. Cancel sits top left for a call with nothing to log.
  2. Five next steps. Send a proposal, schedule a meeting, a follow-up call, send information, or none.
  3. “None” is an answer. A call can be closed without inventing a task to go with it.
  4. Pick a day. The next four days, or Custom for anything further out.
  5. A reminder, spelled out. It says exactly when you’ll hear about it: Tue 30 at 9:00 AM.
  6. Save and complete. One button closes this call and sets the next step.

Next Steps

Testing With People Who Manage Client Work

To be completed

The interactive prototype will be tested with people who manage client-related tasks or delegated work. Testing will focus on whether they can create tasks efficiently, request input, understand task status, find earlier discussions and complete follow-ups without losing context.

What testing finds will shape the next design iterations.

Reflection

From Problems I Had To Problems Teams Share

FollowUp began as an exploration of problems I had run into firsthand, and grew into a broader look at collaborative task management.

Throughout the project, a key consideration is separating my own professional observations from the needs of the wider audience. More user research and usability testing will be important for checking whether these workflows solve problems other small teams actually have.
