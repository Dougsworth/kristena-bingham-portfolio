01

Where it started

As a virtual assistant, I used an in-house CRM every day while it was being built.

I tested it daily, recorded each problem on screen, recommended fixes directly to the CEO and checked every fix. So this redesign didn’t start from a brief. It started from the same three problems coming back again and again.

02

The problem

  • 01
    Tasks that never clear

    Completed follow-ups stayed on the dashboard, so the real to-do list was buried under finished work.

  • 02
    Hand-offs to the wrong person

    Tasks were assigned to the wrong teammate, so the person who should act never saw them.

  • 03
    Notifications that go nowhere

    A notification said something needed attention, but it didn’t open the task it was about.

Each one meant a follow-up could slip. In a small team that chases invoices, a slipped follow-up is usually a slipped payment.

03

Who it’s for

Small teams that chase clients by phone, WhatsApp and email: payments, quotes, renewals and installs, often handled between calls.

Design goals

  • 01
    Make what’s late impossible to miss

    Open on what’s overdue and how much money is waiting on it.

  • 02
    Make logging faster than forgetting

    One line of text, not a form.

  • 03
    Close the loop automatically

    Logging the next step clears the one it replaces.

  • 04
    Show how the team is keeping up

    Plain numbers by week and by person.

04

How it works

From a late follow-up to the next step, in six moves.

  1. Step 01

    See what’s late

    The Today board opens on overdue follow-ups and the money waiting on them.

  2. Step 02

    Log it in one line

    Type the follow-up the way you’d say it. Press N from anywhere.

  3. Step 03

    Check the fields

    Action, client, time and invoice are filled in. Tab into any of them to fix it.

  4. Step 04

    Close the old one

    The late follow-up it replaces is offered up to mark as done.

  5. Step 05

    Make the call

    Call straight from the client panel, with a tip on when they usually pick up.

  6. Step 06

    Set the next step

    After the call, pick the outcome and a day on your phone. The next follow-up is made for you.

07

Key design decisions

Overdue comes first

The board opens on what’s late and how much money is waiting on it, because a slipped follow-up is usually a slipped payment.

Type it the way you’d say it

One line of plain text becomes an action, a client, a time and a linked invoice. Tab jumps into any field to correct it.

Close the loop for you

Logging a new follow-up offers to mark the late one it replaces as done, which fixes the tasks-that-never-clear problem at its source.

Finish the job after the call

On a phone, pick the outcome and a day, and the next step is scheduled before you put it down.

Show why it guessed

Each parsed field names the words it came from, so a wrong guess is easy to spot and one Tab away from fixed.

Built for working through a list

Every action on the board has a key, because follow-ups come in batches, not one at a time.

08

Visual language

Calm and clerical on purpose.

White panels sit on a pale blue-grey. One indigo marks anything you can act on, and rust is kept for what’s late. Money and times are set in a monospaced face so columns of figures line up.

What I’d test next

  • The test

    Log follow-ups with the one-line box and with a standard form, and compare time taken and errors.

  • Who with

    People who manage client follow-ups day to day.

  • What I’d watch

    How often people correct a parsed field, and whether wrong assignments drop.

  • Open questions

    Will people trust the parsed fields without checking each one? What should happen when two clients share a first name?
