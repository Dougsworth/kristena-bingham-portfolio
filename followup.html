01

Background

FollowUp was inspired by my experience using an early-stage internal CRM while supporting client management and operational workflows.

Through daily use, I ran into issues with task ownership, status visibility, navigation and communication between team members. Tasks that needed review or more context also showed how important it is to keep discussions and decisions connected to the work itself.

I used these experiences as the starting point for exploring a simpler way for small teams to create, manage and collaborate on client-related tasks.

02

The problem

Small teams often coordinate many client-related tasks across different people. When task updates, discussions and decisions are scattered, keeping context and working out what needs to happen next takes more effort than it should.

How might we

How might we help small teams quickly capture tasks, collaborate on next steps, and keep a clear record of discussions and updates in one place?

03

Discovery

I began with observations from my own professional experience and secondary research into existing task-management and CRM products.

Where I started

  • 01
    Less effort to capture

    Reducing the effort it takes to create and update tasks.

  • 02
    Discussion stays with the task

    Keeping feedback and discussions connected to individual tasks.

  • 03
    Easier to read at a glance

Comparative analysis

I looked at Todoist, Asana, ClickUp and HubSpot to see how established products handle quick task creation, task discussions, approvals and client activity histories.

04

Key opportunities

Discovery pointed to four areas worth exploring further.

  • 01
    Quick task capture

    Create a task with a simple one-line entry instead of filling in a long form straight away.

  • 02
    Contextual collaboration

    Keep comments, feedback and review requests inside the task they’re about.

  • 03
    Clear progress

    Make ownership and task states, To Do, In Progress, Awaiting Review and Completed, easy to identify.

  • 04
    Centralised client history

    Connect individual tasks and important updates to a chronological client record.

05

The solution

A lightweight client and task manager built on two ideas: capture what needs to happen quickly, and keep the collaboration that follows attached to that work.

  • 01
    Personal dashboard

    A focused view of your assigned tasks and their current status.

  • 02
    One-line log

    Capture a follow-up quickly, then review or change the task details it picked out.

  • 03
    Task discussion

    A dedicated space for comments, feedback, review requests and relevant updates.

  • 04
    Client timeline

    A chronological history of the important tasks and activity for each client.

  • 05
    Contextual notifications

    A notification takes you straight to the task that needs your attention.

06

Key user flow

When a client follow-up needs another team member’s input.

  1. Step 01

    Create the task

    Log the follow-up in a line.

  2. Step 02

    Prepare the follow-up

    Draft what’s going to the client.

  3. Step 03

    Request review

    Ask a teammate for input, from inside the task.

  4. Step 04

    Reviewer is notified

    The notification opens the task itself.

  5. Step 05

    Feedback or approval

    Given in the task’s discussion, next to the work.

  6. Step 06

    Complete the task

    The follow-up goes out and the task closes.

  7. Step 07

    Kept in the client’s history

    The task and its discussion stay on the client timeline.

This flow became central to the design because finishing a task isn’t always a solo action. Sometimes progress depends on another person’s decision or context.

09

Testing and iteration

To be completed.

The interactive prototype will be tested with people who manage client-related tasks or delegated work. Testing will focus on whether they can create tasks efficiently, request input, understand task status, find earlier discussions and complete follow-ups without losing context.

What testing finds will shape the next design iterations.

10

Reflection

FollowUp began as an exploration of problems I had run into firsthand, and grew into a broader look at collaborative task management.

Throughout the project, a key consideration is separating my own professional observations from the needs of the wider audience. More user research and usability testing will be important for checking whether these workflows solve problems other small teams actually have.
