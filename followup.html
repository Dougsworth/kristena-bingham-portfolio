Where it started

As a virtual assistant, I used an in-house CRM every day while it was being built, and I reported its bugs to the CEO. Three problems kept coming back: completed tasks didn’t clear from the dashboard, tasks went to the wrong person, and notifications didn’t open the task they were about. Each one meant a follow-up could slip.

The idea

Put what’s overdue first, and make logging a follow-up one line of text instead of a form. Typing “Call Marsha about invoice 1042 Friday 10am” fills in the action, client, time and linked invoice, and offers to close the follow-up it replaces.

Key design decisions

Overdue comes first

The board opens on what’s late and how much money is waiting on it, because a slipped follow-up is usually a slipped payment.

Type it the way you’d say it

One line of plain text becomes an action, a client, a time and a linked invoice. Tab jumps into any field to correct it.

Close the loop for you

Logging a new follow-up offers to mark the late one it replaces as done, which fixes the tasks-that-never-clear problem at its source.

Finish the job after the call

On a phone, pick the outcome and a day, and the next step is scheduled before you put it down.
