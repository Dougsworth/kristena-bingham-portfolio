Overview

Years Of Rental Data, And One Obvious Gap

The idea grew out of my years working with rental property data at a real estate analytics firm.

I analysed client and property data across rent management systems, resolved discrepancies and QA-checked the reports built from it. Working with that data makes one thing obvious: a price on its own tells a renter very little.

Problem

A Price On Its Own Tells A Renter Very Little

  1. 01No context

    Listings show a price, but not how it compares with similar homes nearby. Renters can’t easily tell a fair price from an inflated one.

  2. 02Hidden monthly costs

    Utilities, water storage and the commute change what a home really costs each month.

  3. 03Local essentials buried

    Water tanks, security and bus times sit in the description, if they’re mentioned at all.

Audience

Renters Around Kingston 6, Comparing A Handful Of Homes On A Budget

Renters in and around Kingston 6, many of them a bus ride from UWI Mona, comparing a handful of homes on a budget.

Design goals

  1. 01Put every price in context

    Show where each home sits against its neighbourhood before anyone calls.

  2. 02Let renters judge for themselves

    The home against its area’s median, not a “good deal” badge.

  3. 03Compare the real monthly cost

    Rent plus utilities and the other monthly bills.

  4. 04Treat local details as data

    Water storage, parking and security as proper fields.

How It Works

From A Search To A Shortlist You Can Defend

  1. Step 01

    Search an area

    Start with a place, like Kingston, then price, bedrooms and type.

  2. Step 02

    Read the map

    Pins are coloured against the median, with area averages on top.

  3. Step 03

    Scan the list

    Photo, rent, bedrooms, bathrooms and size for every home.

  4. Step 04

    Open a home

    The estimated monthly cost, and where the rent sits against the median.

  5. Step 05

    Check the area

    Median rent, its six-month trend, and rent by size.

  6. Step 06

    Compare and save

    Two homes side by side on total cost, and searches that watch for new listings.

Decisions

Key Design Decisions

  1. 01Price in context

    Map pins are coloured by where each home sits against the median, and a listing says how far above or below it is.

  2. 02Show the yardstick

    The fair-price bar names the median it’s measured against: “New Kingston 2 bed median is $170,000 /mo”.

  3. 03The real monthly cost

    A listing and a comparison both lead to a total with utilities and internet, not rent alone.

  4. 04Local details are first-class

    Water tank, parking and a gated compound are tags on the listing, not lines buried in a description.

  5. 05Say the answer in words

    Compare ends with plain sentences: which home costs less each month, and which gives more space.

  6. 06Label estimates as estimates

    Utilities, internet and totals carry “(est.)”, so the total never pretends to be exact.

Visual Language

Clean And Calm

Inter throughout, on white panels with hairline borders and real photos. Green carries prices and actions, and marks the homes below the median on the map. Blue and violet pins mark homes near and above it. A lighter violet is kept for the rent trend and the compare totals.

  2. 02The flow
