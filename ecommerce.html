The brief

A local business needed an online store: somewhere customers could browse what it sells, pick what they want and pay.

As a student team at The University of the West Indies, we took it from research to working software.

  • Research first

    Start from what the business and its customers need, not from a template.

  • Design it properly

    High-fidelity screens that the team could build from.

  • Ship it

    Working software the business could use, not just a prototype.

02

My role

I worked on both sides of the project: the design and the build.

  • 01
    UX research

    I conducted UX research to understand what the platform needed to do.

  • 02
    UI design

    I turned the findings and the project requirements into high-fidelity prototypes in Figma.

  • 03
    Development

    I contributed production code as the team built the designs.

Working on both sides meant designing with the build in mind, and keeping what shipped close to what was designed.

04

The shape of the store

Every online store comes down to the same five steps. Each one has a single job.

  • 01
    Browse

    Products by category, with the price visible before you tap.

  • 02
    Product

    A big picture, the price, the options and one clear button.

  • 03
    Bag

    What you’re buying, delivery or pick-up, and the total before you commit.

  • 04
    Checkout

    Your details, how you’ll pay, and the total one last time.

  • 05
    Order placed

    A clear confirmation, with what happens next.

Simplified illustrations of the store’s journey, with placeholder products and prices.

Design to code

The same person on both sides of the handoff.

In Figma
  • Research findings and requirements, side by side
  • High-fidelity screens for every step of the journey
  • Prototypes the team could click through before building
In code
  • The designs built as working software
  • Python and JavaScript
  • My production code, alongside the team’s

Designing and coding on the same project closed the gap between the two. Questions about how something would be built could be answered while it was still being designed.
